## Why

Beat 3's claim volume is one division: refunds paid per year over the mean
claim, and the mean claim is the lognormal's own mean, `exp(mu + sigma²/2)`,
€494.45 over the tracked fit. The frozen DAMIR fixture has an arithmetic mean
of its own — €847.59, 5,000 cells, one of them €558,803 — that the fitted
curve does not reproduce: the lognormal's tail is lighter than the fixture's
largest cells. So the claim count the page shows (707,858 at the defaults)
would be 412,935 at the sample's own mean, a 42% gap that the page cannot show
today, because the fit artifact carries no arithmetic mean and the model has no
row for it. A reader who redoes the arithmetic from the fixture gets a number
the page never mentions. (The figures in this paragraph are the spec's, not the
page's: the page shows the two means and the two counts, each a mart cell — no
maximum cell, no percentage gap, no tail diagnosis; challenge round 1, #8.)

The page calls the quantity what the artifact already calls its siblings: a
*cell* — a DAMIR cell sums one or more claims, so "the mean reimbursement
cell" is the honest name, as `emp_p50` is "the median reimbursement cell". One
name, `emp_mean`, travels from the artifact row to the parameter row to the
display map, the `emp_p50` precedent (round 1, #3). The branch keeps its
`sample-mean` slug; the page never says "sample mean".

This is a phase, not a fix PR: it changes a tracked artifact's shape (the fit
CSV gains a row, so its strict reader changes with it), adds a parameter and a
formula to the model, and changes what B3.1 and B3.3 render — a data structure,
a write path and the page, the fix-amendment triad (CLAUDE.md → Workflow rules).

## The central constraint

**Every number the page shows today keeps its value; the phase adds a contrast
beside the claim count, never a different claim count.** `fixtures/damir/` is
not re-frozen; `mu`, `sigma`, `n`, the nine decile pairs, `mean_claim`,
`claims` and every downstream output stay at their pinned values on every
scenario; the artifact changes by exactly one row appended as its last line —
the pre-phase file is a byte prefix of the new one — regenerated by
`make fit-damir` over the frozen fixture and byte-equal to the recompute.

## Pinned decisions (do not re-litigate)

- **The artifact gains exactly one row, `emp_mean`, as its last line, six
  decimals, and the writer and the strict reader read one name set.**
  `opendata.fit.Fit` gains `emp_mean` (the sample's arithmetic mean, computed
  in `fit_lognormal` beside `n` — two sample facts that always travel
  together), `write_fit` writes it last (so the pre-phase file is a byte
  prefix), `_fit_field_names` becomes the public tuple `FIT_FIELD_NAMES` that
  the writer, `read_fit` and `tests/test_snapshots.py` all read — one closed
  set, no hand-typed copy — so `read_fit` refuses an old-shape artifact by
  name and a value ≤ 0 by name (satisfies invariants 1, 2, 7). The fixture is
  not re-frozen; the tracked artifact is regenerated by `make fit-damir` and
  the change is a DECISIONS line (the BACKLOG row's "`Freeze:`-style" line).
  *Rejected: a separate `sample_mean()` and a wider `write_fit(fit, gof, mean,
  path)` — a second thing to hand around that is never written without the
  first. Rejected: the row after `n` (round 1, #1) — it breaks the byte-prefix
  property for a grouping the reader never sees.*
- **The model adds a parameter and a formula; no existing entry changes.**
  `fit_parameters` returns a fourth row `emp_mean` (sourced, `_FIT_CITE`, low
  == default == high — the `emp_p50` precedent, so B3.3 draws it as a fixed
  mark); `POINT_FORMULAS` gains `claims_at_mean_cell` (`refunded_eur /
  emp_mean`, `count`) right after `claims`; `cost_model.Fit` gains `emp_mean`,
  lifted by `read_model_fit` (satisfies 3, 4). `emp_mean` is the second
  read-figure row under `Parameter` (whose docstring says "on a slider");
  accepted as the `emp_p50` precedent, with a BACKLOG row whose trigger is a
  third such row (round 1, #10). *Rejected: `mean_claim` as a
  parameter with the range [fit mean .. sample mean] — the formula list would
  lose the fit's derivation and `mu`/`sigma` their reason; a range is two
  computed rows here, never a hand-typed pair.*
- **The contrast is a formula row and a note, not a second claim count.**
  `mean_claim` stays the lognormal mean and `claims` stays the count every
  downstream formula uses, so Beat 4 and the simulator are untouched
  (satisfies 4). The note (in `study/text.py`, data) says only what its two
  cells show: the mean reimbursement cell sits above the fitted mean, so the
  lognormal puts less weight on the largest cells than the sample carries, and
  the count at the mean cell is the lower of the two; no maximum cell, no
  percentage, no decile (the goodness-of-fit table is not rendered, and at
  p70–p90 the fit is the heavier side — round 1, #8). *Rejected: switching
  `claims` to the mean cell — a different page, not a contrast, and a Beat 4
  re-pin.*
- **The page reads the new row from the marts like every other.** B3.1's
  reader already prints every `cost_model_outputs` row of the baseline in
  `FORMULAS` order; `FORMULA_NAMES` and `PARAMETER_NAMES` gain one entry each
  (the closed maps; a name outside them refuses — 9c decision 6); B3.3's
  headline gains a fourth row through `HEADLINE_FORMULAS` (`claims_at_mean_cell`
  after `claims`), read from the outputs mart at the baseline, never retyped,
  the same `Series` shape as the three (satisfies 6). The palette and chart
  types do not change; no new chart.
- **Every new number is typed from the built output.** `tests/pins.py` gains
  `DAMIR_EMP_MEAN`, `DAMIR_FIT_PRE_9H_SHA256`, `emp_mean`'s row,
  `claims_at_mean_cell` per scenario; the row-count and name-set pins grow
  (`COST_PARAM_ROWS` 15 → 16, `COST_OUTPUT_ROWS` 56 → 60, `BEAT3_FORMULA_ROWS`,
  `BEAT3_SOURCED_ROWS`, `BEAT3_HEADLINES` gains the fourth name); no pinned
  quantity's value changes. The `make study` render is re-committed and
  byte-checked.
